# Onboarding-to-follow-up video direction

Updated: 7 September 2026. This supersedes the earlier one-minute-only video proposal.

## Recommended format

Use a hybrid of a simple classroom scenario, actual product screen recordings, and a shared overview of the synthetic participants. Show the real-world intent immediately before the corresponding software operation. Keep the same two professors, courses and four students throughout.

The recording overview currently being prepared is one chapter, not the complete onboarding flow. It replays actual domain-service events from a synthetic store; it is not a screen recording of the ordinary professor/student UI. Capture the ordinary onboarding and delivery UI separately for the finished film.

## Candidate comparison

| Format | Strength | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product screen recording throughout | Shows actual operations clearly | Actor relationships and elapsed classroom time need explanation |
| Classroom scenario animation throughout | Makes people, sequence and time easy to understand | Alone, it does not establish that the software executed the flow |
| Classroom scenario + product capture + shared event overview | Connects intended use to actual software behaviour | Requires consistent labels and transitions between the views |

Select the third format. Use simple diagrams and synthetic labels, without spending time on realistic animated people.

## Provisional 3-minute sequence

| Time | Classroom meaning | On-screen evidence | English caption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–12 s | Two instructors prepare different courses | Cast and course-assignment diagram | Two instructors prepare their course Twins. |
| 12–40 s | Professor A defines how the Twin may teach | Actual onboarding interview and resulting review fields | The instructor defines sources, teaching preferences, and limits. |
| 40–65 s | The instructor makes materials available for use | Actual source registration/upload, permission and processing status; distinguish metadata registration from file ingestion | Approved course materials become the knowledge source. |
| 65–90 s | The instructor checks and authorizes the Twin | Actual preview, review, teaching-profile approval and publication; show blocked states if a gate fails | The instructor reviews the behaviour before release. |
| 90–105 s | Professor B prepares a separate course; students enter | Shortened second-course preparation and student join/consent view | Each student joins an authorized course. |
| 105–135 s | Four students ask their own questions | Shared overview plus readable selected response; use recorded outputs verbatim | Each conversation has its own course context and saved record. |
| 135–150 s | A later teaching opportunity occurs | Explicit Day 1 → Day 2 transition; actual opportunity and permission checks | Time advances in the simulation. |
| 150–170 s | Support is allowed in one course and paused in the other | Actual delivery and paused-course outcome | Continuing support remains under instructor control. |
| 170–180 s | Summarize what the demonstration established | Return to the two-course diagram | From instructor setup to continuing student support. |

Timing is editorial and provisional. Prefer extending a reading moment over speeding up dense text. Keep typing, uploads and waiting short with cuts or explicit speed-up labels. All stages remain present; repetitive clicks need not be shown in real time.

## Presentation and evidence boundaries

- Persistent label: “Synthetic classroom scenario · Recorded software execution”.
- During the custom overview: “Service-event replay · Deterministic baseline”.
- The classroom diagram explains intended use, not evidence of a real class deployment.
- Two professors and four students are synthetic actors. The replay uses sequential actions in one isolated store; simultaneous display is not a concurrent-load benchmark.
- No learning-effect, mastery or instructor-style fidelity claim follows from the demonstration.
- The current overview scripts its practice opportunities explicitly. Do not describe them as autonomously inferred from measured learning needs.
- The overview's initial approved synthetic sources do not prove onboarding, upload or approval worked end to end. The full film needs those distinct operations captured from the product.
- Two students per course do not meet the five-learner cohort reporting threshold. The overview is a recording-only synthetic observer view, not a new professor entitlement to private student conversations.
- A paused opportunity may remain pending. Say “No delivery while paused”, not “Cancelled”, unless the actual record reports cancellation.
- Historical model outputs can appear only as separately labelled historical examples; do not splice them into this execution as if newly generated.

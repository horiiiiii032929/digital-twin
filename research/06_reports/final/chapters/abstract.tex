\begin{abstract}
This project designs and implements an instructor-configurable Digital Twin
that answers course questions and initiates support between student requests.
Reviewed materials, teaching preferences and objectives are connected to
evidence selection, persistent learner records, action planning and in-app
delivery. Comparative studies examine evidence interfaces, guarded planning,
model allocation and learner-estimator/timing combinations. A typed
evidence interface improved grounded answers from 63.73\% to 89.42\% on one
development fold. On a different fresh-case evaluation, the retained BM25
fallback met the full grounding criteria in 63.25\% of answerable cases, below
its acceptance threshold. Integrated trials using external large language models (LLMs) exercised 24 histories
over 30 virtual days, including proactive delivery, consent changes and
restart. A subsequent objective-scoped lifecycle correction reduced unsupported
goal completions from 12 to zero in matched 72-history arms. Mean final
simulator mastery did not increase relative to the previous completion logic. The results establish a working research prototype and
specific design findings; they also expose a gap between controlled execution
and individualized teaching. The evaluations used synthetic students and teaching profiles; effects on
real-student learning and fidelity to the actual instructor remain untested.

\end{abstract}

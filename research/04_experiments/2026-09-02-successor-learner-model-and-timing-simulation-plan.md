# Successor learner model and intervention timing: network-free simulation

Date: 2026-09-02

Run ID: `successor-learner-timing-simulation-001`

Status: plan frozen before any output was opened; network-free; no provider,
paid, private-data, human-study, or release authorization

Source study:
[`docs/research/sota-autonomous-digital-twin-independent-study.md`](../../docs/research/sota-autonomous-digital-twin-independent-study.md)
and the decision document's hypotheses H1, H2, H4, and H5.

## Question

Under identical eligibility rules (consent, quiet hours, frequency ceiling,
same-concept cooldown), does replacing the current count-based learner belief
and constant +24 h intervention timing with a calibrated estimator and a
value-based, spacing-aware timing policy produce better learner-state
calibration and fewer wasted interventions on simulated learners with hidden
state, and does the estimator ranking hold across two simulator families?

This is the smallest experiment in the decision document that can reject the
forward-model and calibrated-estimator components without any model call.

## Hypothesis

- H1 (belief): a two-state Bayesian Knowledge Tracing estimator with
  forgetting, and a logistic Performance Factors estimator, will both have a
  lower Brier score against hidden mastery than the evidence-count baseline,
  on both simulator families, with the paired difference's lower confidence
  bound above zero.
- H2 (timing): the value-based policy will have a lower wasted-intervention
  rate than constant timing at equal or higher assessed-follow-up fraction,
  without any frequency, cooldown, or quiet-hour violation.
- H4 (forward model risk): if the estimator is poorly calibrated, the
  value-based policy will not beat conditional timing; this is the expected
  failure and is reported rather than tuned away.
- Prediction registered before running: BKT and PFA beat counts by at least
  0.02 Brier; value timing reduces wasted interventions by at least 5 points;
  the ranking is the same on both families; the prediction may be wrong on the
  logistic family for BKT.

## Method

- Dataset: synthetic learners generated by
  `src/digital_twin/evaluation/learner_simulator.py`. Six personas, two
  transition families (`bkt-like`, `logistic-like`), eight concepts with a
  prerequisite chain, thirty virtual days at one decision per day, six
  development seeds per persona and family for estimator parameter fitting,
  and twenty held-out seeds per persona and family for scoring. Development
  and held-out seed ranges are disjoint and frozen here: development
  `1000-1005`, held-out `2000-2019`.
- Baseline: `count` estimator (Laplace-smoothed proportion correct with the
  current `n / (n + 2)` confidence rule and no decay) with `constant` timing
  (one wake-up every 24 h while a goal is active, the current behaviour).
- Variants: estimators `bkt` and `pfa`; timing policies `conditional`
  (intervene only on a replan predicate: stalled or low-and-uncertain
  estimate) and `value` (intervene only when the analytic forward model's
  expected gain over no action exceeds a margin, with spacing).
- Bounds: `oracle` timing that reads hidden state, and `never` timing, to
  express results as a fraction of the measurable gap.
- Eligibility shared by every condition: consent on, quiet hours 22:00 to
  08:00 local, at most 3 messages per 7 days, 24 h same-concept cooldown.
- Metrics, chosen before the run: mean daily Brier score and expected
  calibration error of the estimate against hidden mastery; AUROC of the
  estimate for the next assessed outcome; wasted-intervention rate (message
  sent when hidden mastery is at or above 0.85 or the learner is not
  receptive); assessed-follow-up fraction within 48 h; messages per learner;
  eligibility violations; final hidden mastery as the simulator's own effect
  proxy; runtime per learner. Paired by (persona, family, seed) with a
  1,000-resample bootstrap interval on the paired difference.
- Risks: the estimator could be tuned to one simulator family (mitigated by
  two families and a separate development seed range); the receptivity model
  is a hypothesis (reported as such); the simulator is text free so nothing
  about generation is measured; effects on real learners are out of scope.

## Failure-case classification

Each failed prediction is classified as: simulator defect, estimator
mis-specification, policy defect, eligibility defect, or harness defect.

## Results

Recorded in
[`research/05_evaluation/successor-learner-timing-simulation-001-results.md`](../05_evaluation/successor-learner-timing-simulation-001-results.md).
Attempt 001 was declared invalid on a simulator defect before analysis;

## Decision

Go Deeper. BKT-with-forgetting behind the estimator interface and the
value-based timing policy are the successor's default hypotheses; not
selected for release; provider-backed dimensions remain open.

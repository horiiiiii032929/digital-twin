# Conditional instructional revision

## Decision and alternatives

V12 forcibly rewrote every draft and introduced a complete new-concept solution
in an adequate initial question. V13 preserved40/40 adequate controls and repaired
all8 fresh disclosure controls, but repaired only29/32 old defects. A draft's
question/private action is not authoritative evidence of the appropriate move:
its restriction prevented a requested application and an ordinary privacy
explanation. A separate false third-party boundary also remained. Preserve both
failed runs and their gates.

Compare V14, one conditional Sol response, with these exposed development
references. Sol may keep the original draft unchanged or propose a repair.
A structured wrapper supplies keep/repair, a bounded primary fault category,
a brief observable diagnosis, the current target concept, a proposed teaching
move, and an optional typed replacement. Keep requires no fault, preserve move
and a null replacement. Repair requires a non-null replacement consistent with
the proposed move. Instructional moves allow complete or mixed supported/missing
responses; missing details remain separately represented. The original draft's
action does not constrain a repair. Current-concept attempts and the approved
profile, rather than total message count, govern the requested pedagogical move.

The prediction is preservation without compulsory rewriting, while allowing
correction of an erroneous refusal or under-helpful question. The alternatives
are V12's unconditional revision, V13's draft-action ceiling, or an additional
critic request before revision. No evidence yet justifies the extra call. The
single wrapper is a fallible joint proposal: fault/move fields may rationalize a
bad replacement and are not independently validated diagnoses or permissions.
Source association and schema consistency do not prove semantic correctness.
No subject-specific branches, templates for observed answers or retries are added.

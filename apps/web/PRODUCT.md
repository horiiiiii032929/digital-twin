# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Professors and teaching staff configure a Course Digital Twin before it is
available to students. They check source permissions, tutor policy, preview
evidence, and release blockers.

Students use only an assigned course's current published release. They need a
focused tutoring conversation, clear safe failures, and inspectable citation
lineage without access to professor configuration or other courses.

## Product Purpose

Course Digital Twin connects professor-controlled configuration to a grounded
student tutor. Success means an instructor can understand and approve the
release boundary, while an assigned student can ask a course question, receive
an answer or safe action from that exact release, and inspect the supporting
source version and locator.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, and accountable. Both role surfaces should feel like one mature
grounded-AI product: direct enough for repeated use, quiet enough for careful
work, and transparent about evidence, permissions, release state, and failure.

## Anti-references

This should not feel like a generic AI chat demo, a decorative SaaS landing page,
or an opaque automation panel. Avoid oversized hero treatment, ornamental card
grids, purple-blue gradient AI branding, hidden release blockers, and vague
"magic" language around tutor behavior.

## Design Principles

- Lead with the role's current task: release readiness for professors and the
  active assigned-course conversation for students.
- Make evidence auditable: professor decisions connect to the release gate, and
  student answers connect to validated citation and source-version lineage.
- Keep role boundaries explicit: professors govern releases; students can use
  only their assigned current release.
- Prefer dense clarity over decoration: the surface should support scanning,
  comparison, and repeated review work.
- Protect sensitive data by default: synthetic examples are the visible norm,
  and the student prototype labels its local synthetic-account boundary.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast and keyboard-operable review controls. Motion should be
limited to state feedback and respect reduced-motion preferences. Color should
never be the only indicator of status; release blockers, course availability,
tutor actions, and citation selection need text and icon support. Use clear
labels for assistive technology and avoid private course details in examples.
